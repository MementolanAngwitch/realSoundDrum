What this actually is

A clamped circular sheet, given physical properties, hit by a striker with physical properties of its own. Every sound is solved from the numbers in the panel. Nothing is a sample. The primitive is deliberately unnamed — fitting it to a particular instrument is a separate job, done afterwards, against a model that doesn't already assume the answer.

The chain

Frequency. ω² = (T k² + D k⁴)/σ_eff, with D = E h³/12(1−ν²). The k⁴ term is bending stiffness and it is not a correction: on a 10 mil Mylar head it is already half the restoring force by j = 120, and 98% on a steel foil. A model with only T k² can only ever be a drum. The two terms are kept apart because tension modulation scales only the first.

Air. The sheet drags a layer of air with it, weighted toward the low modes, which drags the Bessel ratios 1 : 1.34 : 1.67 : 1.98 toward harmonic. That pull is why a drum reads as pitched at all. Checked against measured timpani partial ratios: 3.7% error, against 21% for an ideal membrane.

The cavity is one compliance — a rank-one stiffness update, solved by a secular equation whose roots interlace the uncoupled ones. Only the breathing modes couple, and with a second sheet it splits that mode into a pair.

The strike. Contact time is not a knob: the dent spreads at the sheet's wave speed, so τ = 2w/c, floored by the striker's own softness. Tip radius is the single biggest control on brightness.

Damping is a constant loss factor, σ = σ₀ + η ω/2 — a polymer loses a fixed fraction of the strain energy per cycle, not a fixed amount per second.

Radiation is the Rayleigh far field of a baffled disc, which for a clamped mode has a closed form. On axis you hear only the breathing modes; off axis the petal modes stop cancelling. Where the sheet's phase speed reaches the speed of sound the expression is 0/0 and its limit is large — a real radiation peak, and why a cymbal is loud and a drumhead is not.

Where it stops being honest

The mode shapes are the membrane ones, so a stiffness-dominated sheet is approximated — watch Stiffness at top. The stretch integral only converges once the band resolves the striker tip, so the tip is floored at what the retained modes can see. Model range tells you when the pitch bend hit its clamp, past which small-strain theory is no longer telling the truth. Limiter drive is the recording chain, not the sheet. Shell flexure, the room, lug asymmetry and snare wires are not modelled.

Snare wires are the one deliberate omission. The contact model works, but the cavity is treated as a single uniform pressure, which cannot drive the far sheet at any angular order above zero — so the wires would be rattling against one slow breathing mode. A control that does nothing is worse than no control.
